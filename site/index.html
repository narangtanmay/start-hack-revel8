The problem

Attackers hold a conversation. Most tests send one email.

Social engineering is how most breaches begin — not through a firewall, but through a person who was asked the right thing at the right moment.

Yet teams still test it by hand: a template, sent once, graded later. It doesn't follow up, it doesn't adapt, and it looks nothing like a real adversary.

A real attacker researches, picks a persona, and stays in character across days and channels. To defend against that, you have to be able to test against that.

How it works

Four stages. No script.

01

Research

Public sources only. Every fact is checked against its source before the agent may use it.

02

Brief & memory

The verified facts become hooks, and what worked against this target before is remembered.

03

Casting

The agent invents one believable person, with a role, a reason to write, and one small ask.

04

Session

It writes, sends, reads the reply and decides the next move, touch after touch, in character.

One persona. Every channel.

The same character follows up over email, chat and a live phone call. Authorised simulations against consenting targets, with no human in the loop.
