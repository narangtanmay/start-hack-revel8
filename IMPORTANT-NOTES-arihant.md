## 1. The 40-point game: it's the DECISIONS, not the writing

Haha yes — and that's the **actual 40-point game** right there. The founders just handed us the answer to the *"what separates 38/40 from 20/40"* question: **it's not the writing, it's the sequencing decisions.** Anyone's LLM can write a decent phishing email. The hard, human part is the **orchestration**:

> "I emailed them, they opened it but didn't reply — a real me would now **call** and say *'hey, did you get my email about X?'*"

That judgment — **which move, which channel, when to push, when to back off** — is what a seasoned social engineer does and what a naive bot can't.

---

## 2. Two more things the founders said

**a) Don't make the agent super strict / over-fitted to the harness.**
They don't want a brittle agent that games the simulation mechanics or rigidly follows a fixed script against the API. It should behave like a *real, flexible* attacker — adaptive, reading the situation — **not rule-bound to the eval harness.**

**b) Everyone can build an orchestrator. They want something NEW.**
The standard *research → plan → contact → adapt* pipeline is table stakes — **every team will have one.** The founders are explicitly looking for a **novel differentiator**: an original idea/angle that stands out, not just the obvious loop.

---

## 3. What this means for us (Arihant's take)

- Put our best effort into the **decision policy** — *perceive → update our belief about the target → choose the human move + record why* — not just the copy.
- Make decisions **human-like**: use the last touch as the warrant for the next ("following up on my email…"), escalate pressure *gradually*, switch channel on silence, back off when suspicion rises.
- Keep the agent **general and adaptive** — don't over-fit to the harness.
- 🧠 **Open question for the team → where is our "something new"?**
  Let's each bring one original angle that *isn't* just a standard orchestrator. Ideas very welcome — drop them below or in the group.
